# A Cursor for Product Managers
## Problem space and product design for an AI-native PM work OS

*Working draft — design doc, not a PRD. Structure: what "Cursor for X" actually means → the problem space → design thesis → precise product concepts → architecture on a pi-style harness → wedge, metrics, riskiest assumptions.*

---

## 1. What Cursor actually is (and why copying it is harder than it looks)

Cursor gets described as "AI + editor," which misses why it won. Decomposed, it's four things:

1. **It inherited a machine-readable source of truth.** The repo already existed before Cursor showed up. Every generation is grounded in the actual codebase, not in the model's vibes about what the codebase probably contains.
2. **Output arrives as diffs against that truth.** A diff is cheap to verify, granular to accept or reject, and always undoable via git. Nobody has to trust a wall of generated code; they judge a delta.
3. **Verification is the native interaction, not an afterthought.** Engineers already review diffs all day. Cursor delivered AI output in the exact form its users were already expert at judging. The tool didn't ask users to learn a new way to verify — it met them at their existing verification skill.
4. **It lives where the work happens.** No copy-paste across a tool boundary. Suggestions land inside the file being edited.

Now the translation problem: **PMs have no repo.** The PM's "codebase" — problems, evidence, decisions, commitments, rationale — is smeared across Jira, Slack, Confluence/Notion, call recordings, dashboards, spreadsheets, and human memory. So a Cursor for PMs cannot begin with the editor. It has to begin by *materializing the source of truth that Cursor got for free*. Call that the **Product Brain**. Every other concept in this document is downstream of it.

One more translation worth being precise about: engineers verify by reading diffs; PMs verify by checking **provenance** — who said this, where, when, and how many times. So the PM-equivalent of the diff is the **cited claim plus tracked change**. That pairing is the atom of the entire UX, and most of the design follows from taking it seriously.

## 2. The problem space

### 2.1 The job

A PM's actual output is decisions and alignment. Documents are the medium, not the product. Inputs are scattered signals: customer calls, support tickets, analytics movements, sales notes, Slack threads, engineering constraints. Functionally, the PM is a human ETL pipeline plus a synthesis engine plus a broadcast system — and none of those three functions is owned by any tool they use.

Stated as a job-to-be-done: *"When signals accumulate across my tools faster than I can process them, I want to turn them into defensible decisions and get everyone moving on them, so the team builds the right thing and my claims hold up when challenged."* The functional job is synthesis and broadcast. The emotional job is confidence — knowing your claims will survive scrutiny. The social job is being seen as the person who knows what's going on. A design that serves the functional job but damages the emotional and social ones (e.g., by making the PM present AI-generated claims they can't back) will be rejected regardless of how much time it saves.

### 2.2 The pains, stated as behaviors

Mom Test discipline applies: these are recurring observable behaviors, not opinions about tooling. Watch a PM for a week and four loops repeat — reconstructing context before writing anything, copy-pasting between tools to synthesize, chasing status, and re-explaining the same thing at different altitudes.

1. **The context reconstruction loop.** Before producing any artifact, the PM re-gathers what the organization already knows: search Slack, reread call notes, pull metrics, check Jira. The same context is rebuilt for the PRD, then rebuilt again for the exec update, then again for the QBR. This is pure assembly tax, paid repeatedly on the same material.
2. **The seams problem.** Feedback lives in support tools and call recordings; plans live in Jira/Linear; narrative lives in docs; measurement lives in analytics. Each vendor keeps improving *inside* its silo. The PM's actual job — moving meaning *between* silos — is owned by no tool, so it's done by hand, in the PM's head and clipboard.
3. **The verification tax.** Generic AI (a ChatGPT tab, Notion AI) drafts plausible artifacts with zero access to the org's actual evidence. The PM must then fact-check every claim, which frequently costs more than writing from scratch. Worse: errors that slip through get presented under the PM's name, and PM credibility is the career asset. This is why "AI writes your PRD" tools see enthusiastic trial and quiet abandonment.
4. **Decision amnesia.** Decisions happen in meetings and threads; rationale is never captured as an object. Three months later the same debate reopens, or a new stakeholder relitigates it. "Why did we..." questions are answered by archaeology or by whoever has the best memory — and the answer changes depending on who you ask.
5. **Alignment broadcast overhead.** The same underlying truth must be re-rendered per audience — execs, engineering, sales/CS, customers — at different altitudes and in different vocabularies. Each rendering is hand-made and drifts from the others over time, so the org is perpetually slightly misaligned about what was actually agreed.
6. **Stale prioritization.** Frameworks (RICE and friends) get applied to whatever evidence happens to be at hand. The evidence base is partial and aging, so prioritization becomes theater performed on stale data, and everyone in the room quietly knows it.
7. **Role anxiety (the meta-pain).** PMs fear AI reduces them to verifiers of machine output. Any tool that produces walls of unaccountable text feeds this fear directly. Adoption depends on the tool visibly *elevating judgment* — moving PM time from assembly to decisions — and saying so in its interface language.

### 2.3 Root cause, not symptoms

Pains 1, 3, 4, 5, and 6 are symptoms. Pain 2 is structural: **the source of truth for product knowledge doesn't exist as an artifact.** No repo → every artifact requires fresh assembly (1), generation can't be grounded (3), decisions have nowhere durable to live (4), audiences can't render from shared truth (5), and prioritization has no live evidence base (6). Fix the structural cause and the symptoms become features you can build; attack the symptoms individually and you build five disconnected point tools.

### 2.4 Why incumbents are structurally stuck

**Silo gravity:** each vendor's AI is scoped to its own data — Atlassian's AI to the Atlassian suite, Notion AI to Notion, feedback-intelligence tools (Enterpret, Dovetail, Cycle) to feedback. The feedback tools get closest, but they stop at insights: they don't connect evidence forward into decisions, artifacts, and audience communication, which is where the PM's week actually goes. **Business-model gravity:** a ticketing vendor has no incentive to become the neutral synthesis layer sitting above its competitors' tools. **Generic AI** has the inverse problem: universal but contextless, hence the verification tax. The synthesis layer wants to be a neutral, cross-tool product whose entire job is the seams — a position nobody's data-model center of gravity lets them occupy.

**Cost of nobody building it:** PMs keep paying hours per week of assembly tax, org-level "why" knowledge keeps evaporating, and PM AI adoption stalls at drafting emails.

## 3. Design thesis

Three commitments, each traceable to named pains:

1. **Materialize the repo.** Build the Product Brain: a structured, versioned object graph with provenance — not a wiki, not a doc pile — assembled by agents and curated by the PM. (Attacks pains 1, 2, 4, 6.)
2. **Invert the verification tax.** The system never emits unaccountable prose. Every generated claim is either cited — traceable to source in two taps — or explicitly marked as an assumption. Verification stops being a post-hoc slog over a wall of text and becomes the primary interaction: granular, cheap, and applied at the point of maximum leverage. (Attacks pains 3 and 7.)
3. **Agents assemble, the PM judges.** Autonomy is tiered; nothing ever leaves the workspace without the PM initiating it. The product's language is "ready for your judgment," never "done." (Attacks pain 7, and builds the trust everything else depends on.)

The spine of the system is the value chain **Signals → Insights → Artifacts → Alignment**. The product's working surfaces map 1:1 onto that chain, and all of them read from and write to the Brain.

## 4. The Product Brain

### 4.1 Object model

Kept deliberately small — every additional entity type is cognitive load for a non-technical user and a tax on every surface that renders it.

- **Signal** — an atomic piece of raw evidence: a quote from a call, a support ticket, a metric movement, a Slack thread. Carries source, timestamp, actor, and segment. Signals are never edited, only linked.
- **Insight** — a synthesized claim supported by one or more signals ("SMB admins churn during permission setup"). Carries a confidence level derived from evidence breadth, diversity, and recency.
- **Problem** — a durable statement of user or business pain that insights attach to. Problems are the stable spine; insights and signals churn beneath them.
- **Bet** — a chosen response to a problem: hypothesis, scope, owner, target metrics, status. (Deliberately "bet," not "initiative" — the word keeps the hypothesis framing alive.)
- **Decision** — a resolved question: the chosen option, alternatives considered, rationale (linked to insights), decider, date, and a revisit-by date.
- **Artifact** — any rendered document (PRD, one-pager, roadmap view, stakeholder update). An artifact is a *view compiled from the graph*, versioned, not a free-floating text file.
- Supporting types: **Persona/Segment**, **Metric**, **Audience** (used by lenses, §5.3).

Relationships do the real work: signals *support* insights; insights *attach to* problems; bets *respond to* problems; decisions *resolve questions on* bets and *cite* insights as rationale; metrics *measure* bets; artifacts *render* any of the above.

### 4.2 Mechanics that make it trustworthy

- **Provenance on everything.** Click any insight → the verbatim signals beneath it → the original source. Two taps from any claim to its receipts. This single mechanic is what makes generated artifacts presentable under the PM's name.
- **Freshness and confidence are visible.** "Supported by 6 signals, newest 4 months old, all from two accounts" is displayed, not hidden. Insights decay; staleness is flagged rather than silently trusted.
- **Versioned belief.** The brain has history. "What did we believe in March, and what changed it?" is a query, not archaeology. This also makes every action reversible, which makes trust cheap.
- **Curation, not data entry.** The PM never fills in forms. All writes to the brain arrive as agent proposals; the PM confirms, edits, merges, or dismisses. Human-confirmed entities carry a visible badge and higher weight in synthesis and drafting.
- **Contradictions are surfaced, never merged silently.** A new signal that conflicts with a confirmed insight generates a flagged proposal ("this contradicts X — weaken it, split it, or dismiss?"), because silent overwrites are how a brain loses its owner's trust exactly once, permanently.

## 5. The five surfaces

Each surface maps to a stage of the value chain. Everything funnels judgment into one place (the Review Queue) rather than scattering approvals across the app.

### 5.1 Signals Inbox *(Signals → Insights)*

Connectors — meeting recorder, support tool, chosen Slack channels, Jira/Linear, analytics, CRM notes, plus email-forwarding and paste-in as the universal fallback — stream raw material to an ingestion agent that dedupes, clusters, and proposes **at the insight level, not the raw-signal level**: "14 new signals this week → 2 new insights proposed, 1 existing insight strengthened, 1 contradiction flagged." Triage is one-tap: confirm / edit / merge / dismiss. Cadence is digest-based (daily or weekly, PM's choice), never interrupt-driven.

One flow deserves first-class treatment: **Interview Debrief.** Drop in a recording or notes after a customer call and get structured proposed signals and insights, pre-tagged to existing problems, within minutes — while motivation is at its peak and memory is fresh. This is discovery synthesis at the exact moment PMs already want to do it and never have time for.

### 5.2 Artifact Studio *(Insights → Artifacts)*

A document editor where documents compile from the brain. Creation starts from an object ("from this Bet → draft a PRD" / "→ draft a one-pager for the exec review") or from plain-language intent. The draft arrives with:

- **Inline citations** on every grounded claim — tap to see the evidence.
- An explicit **Assumptions block** for anything the brain can't support, worded as assumptions ("we assume enterprise admins will accept SSO-only auth — no evidence either way"), never smuggled in as fact.
- **Open questions surfaced**, not papered over. A draft that says "the brain has nothing on pricing sensitivity here" is more valuable than one that invents something plausible.

All AI edits render as **tracked changes** — accept or reject per suggestion, exactly the reviewing skill PMs already have from Google Docs and Word. And the binding is **two-way**: edit a grounded fact in the document and the system asks "update the underlying Bet/Metric too?" so documents and brain cannot silently diverge. Artifacts export anywhere (Confluence, Notion, Docs, PDF, Jira description fields) — the workspace is the source of truth, not a prison.

### 5.3 Audience Lenses *(Artifacts → Alignment)*

Define each audience once — exec, engineering, sales/CS, customers: its altitude, vocabulary, what it cares about, its cadence. Any bet, roadmap, or update then renders per lens from the same underlying truth. When the truth changes, each lens shows a **pending diff** ("since your last exec update: 2 changes — one bet slipped, one metric turned green"), so re-broadcasting becomes review rather than rewriting. Sending anything is always PM-initiated, always with preview.

### 5.4 Rituals and the Review Queue *(the standing-agent layer)*

No workflow builder — workflow builders are where non-technical users go to feel stupid. Instead, a **gallery of pre-built rituals** the PM switches on:

- **Monday Synthesis** — what changed in the brain last week, what needs judgment, what went stale.
- **Stakeholder Update drafts** — per lens, on its cadence.
- **Roadmap Drift Watch** — execution reality in Jira/Linear vs. promises in artifacts; flags divergence before stakeholders find it.
- **Metric Watch** — movements on bet metrics, linked to the bets they affect.
- **Stale Evidence Sweep** — confirmed insights whose evidence has aged out.

Every ritual output lands in the single **Review Queue** as a proposal — nothing auto-sends, ever. Tuning happens by conversation and by example ("shorter; drop the churn section; lead with risks"), and each ritual maintains a **plain-language description of itself** that visibly updates when tuned — so the PM always knows what their staff is doing, without reading configuration.

### 5.5 Ask the Brain *(cross-cutting)*

Plain-language Q&A grounded in the graph, available everywhere including inside the Studio while writing. Three properties are non-negotiable: **citations** on every answer, **honest confidence** ("2 signals, both from one account — thin"), and **honest absence** ("no evidence on this — add it to the research backlog?"). Refusing to invent is a feature; it's the moment the product proves it's different from the ChatGPT tab. Answers can be pinned into artifacts as cited claims.

**Cross-cutting: decision capture.** A listening agent over meeting transcripts and threads proposes decision records — "sounds like you decided to cut SSO from v1; log it with this rationale?" One tap creates the Decision object. Future relitigation gets a link instead of a meeting.

## 6. Interaction and trust model (the "no CLI, no slash-commands" translation)

Take the constraint seriously rather than cosmetically. What the terminal gives developers is discoverability-by-memorization and composability. PMs need the same power delivered through visible affordances and ordinary language:

- **Three autonomy tiers.** Tier 1, silent: reading, fetching, clustering — internal and reversible. Tier 2, propose-then-approve: every brain write, every artifact edit. Tier 3, PM-initiated only: anything that leaves the workspace — sending updates, creating tickets, emailing, publishing. The tiers are a product promise, stated in onboarding and never violated.
- **Every capability is a visible affordance on an object.** Buttons and menus in context: "Draft…", "Find evidence", "Compare to last quarter", "Render for execs". Plain-language input is always available, never required, and never syntax-sensitive. The system suggests next actions ("this bet has no success metric — define one?") so capability is discovered through use, not documentation.
- **Chat is a side channel; outcomes are objects.** Any conversation that matters materializes as a proposal, a draft, or a brain update. Nothing important lives only in an ephemeral chat scroll.
- **Agent legibility without jargon.** While working, agents narrate in human terms — "read 14 tickets, 3 relevant; checked June activation numbers" — with expandable receipts. The product's vocabulary is the PM's: evidence, insight, bet, decision, draft. The words "prompt," "context," "tokens," "agent run" appear nowhere in the interface.
- **Undo everywhere.** The brain and every artifact are versioned; every accept is reversible. When mistakes are cheap, trust gets extended readily.
- **Async by default.** Long jobs run in the background and land in the Review Queue with a notification. The PM is never held hostage by a spinner.

## 7. The two adoption killers

### 7.1 Cold start

A brain product with an empty brain loses to a ChatGPT tab on day one. So: onboarding is *connect 2–3 sources* (meeting recorder first — richest signal per unit of setup — plus the ticket tool and one docs space). Overnight, the system builds a **draft brain**: proposed problems, insights, and active bets inferred from existing docs, tickets, and recent calls. The first session is a 20-minute guided **correction**: "here are the 7 problems I think you're working on — fix me." People are dramatically better editors than authors; correcting a wrong draft beats authoring into a void, and the correction session doubles as training in the product's core interaction (judging proposals). The first-week win must be a ritual output — a Monday Synthesis or drafted update — that would visibly have cost hours.

### 7.2 Review fatigue

If every signal pings, the Review Queue becomes a second inbox and dies. Mitigations, in priority order: propose at the insight level (§5.1); batch to digest cadence; **confidence-tiered handling** — high-confidence, low-stakes updates auto-apply with an audit badge and one-tap revert, so only genuinely judgment-worthy items ask for attention; auto-expire stale proposals rather than letting them rot. Treat *median queue depth* and *time-to-clear* as first-class product metrics, watched as seriously as engagement. And name the failure mode on the other side: automation bias. As trust grows, review attention decays — which is precisely when a bad claim slips into an exec deck. The citation-first UI is the standing countermeasure: verification never requires effort, so spot-checking stays habitual.

## 8. Architecture on a pi-style harness

### 8.1 What pi gives you

pi (pi.dev, Mario Zechner) is a deliberately minimal open-source agent harness: a tiny core with four tools — read, write, edit, bash — an agent loop and multi-provider LLM layer shipped as composable packages, JSONL session persistence, and an extension system that can persist state into sessions. It's built to be the substrate for *your own* agent (it's what OpenClaw sits on), and its philosophy is that agents extend themselves by writing code rather than by accumulating built-in features.

### 8.2 The consequential design move: the Brain is literally a repo

pi's grain is the filesystem. So don't fight it — **make the Product Brain a git-backed corpus of typed markdown/YAML documents**: `problems/`, `insights/`, `signals/`, `decisions/`, `bets/`, `audiences/`, with entity links and metadata in frontmatter, plus a derived index (embeddings + graph) rebuilt from the files. The consequences are worth the whole architecture:

- **Agents need no bespoke database tooling.** Read/write/edit over structured files is pi's native surface; the harness works on the Brain the way it works on a codebase.
- **Versioned belief, diffs, history, and undo come from git for free.** "What did we believe in March" is `git log`. Nobody builds an audit system; it's already there.
- **Agent proposals are branches; PM approval is a merge.** The Review Queue is, mechanically, a pull-request queue — rendered as one-tap proposal cards, never as git. This makes the product "Cursor for PMs" all the way down: the same substrate and verification mechanics, with a rendering layer that speaks PM instead of developer.
- **Auditability is plain text.** The full history of what agents proposed and what the PM approved is inspectable on disk — which matters enormously to enterprise and regulated buyers, and is a genuine differentiator against SaaS-blackbox competitors.

The GUI is then a rendering layer over the repo: it displays entities, proposals, artifacts, and lenses; the PM never sees a file path, a branch name, or a terminal. Same trick Cursor pulled — git underneath, humane surface on top — one abstraction level higher.

### 8.3 Agent roster and tool discipline

A small roster of specialized sessions on the shared harness, sharing tools rather than each being bespoke:

- **Ingestion** — connectors → proposed signals and insights (clustering, dedupe, tagging).
- **Synthesis** — contradiction detection, confidence updates, the Monday brief.
- **Drafting** — artifact compilation under a hard citation constraint.
- **Q&A** — retrieval over the index + grounded answering with confidence reporting.
- **Watchdog** — drift, staleness, metric anomalies.

Shared toolset: brain **read** and **propose-write** (the only write tool agents have creates proposal branches — direct writes to main do not exist for agents, enforced at the tool layer, not by prompt), connector fetchers, the artifact compiler, the lens renderer. Rituals are scheduled agent runs defined as extensions; the "tune by conversation" UX works because the agent can rewrite its own ritual definition — pi's self-extension idea pointed at ritual configs rather than the harness — with the PM approving the diff to the ritual's plain-language self-description.

### 8.4 Evals from day one

The north-star quality metric is **verification cost**: minutes of PM attention per accepted unit of work, at constant-or-better accuracy. Instrument it from the first prototype: proposal correction rate, citation validity (spot-check sampling), edit distance on accepted drafts, end-to-end time from signal to accepted artifact. Every model or prompt change ships against these numbers, because the entire product thesis is that verification gets cheap — if it doesn't, nothing else matters.

## 9. Wedge, moat, non-goals

**Wedge.** Don't launch the OS; launch the loop that builds the brain as a byproduct. Strongest candidate: **discovery synthesis** — Interview Debrief + Signals Inbox + Monday Synthesis + Ask the Brain. It's high-frequency, the pain is acute, it produces visible value every single week, and every confirmed insight compounds. Artifact Studio and Lenses are the expansion once the brain is rich enough that grounded drafting beats a blank ChatGPT tab by an obvious margin. The tempting alternative wedge — stakeholder updates — is a scheduled, dreaded task, but on an empty brain it underperforms generic AI, so it sequences second, arriving exactly when the brain makes it unbeatable.

**Moat.** The curated, versioned brain. Switching cost grows with every confirmed insight and logged decision; the artifact-quality gap versus contextless AI widens as the brain deepens. The data is the product; the agents are replaceable commodity parts on top of it.

**Non-goals.** Not a ticket tracker — Jira/Linear remain the systems of execution, linked and mirrored, never replaced. Not a wiki replacement — it exports into them. Not an autopilot — nothing leaves without the PM, by product promise. Not a chat assistant — chat is a side channel; the object graph is the product.

## 10. Success metrics and riskiest assumptions

**Activation:** first corrected draft-brain session completed *and* first ritual output accepted, within week one.

**North star:** verification cost trending down at stable accuracy. Supporting: percentage of agent proposals accepted — as a *target band*, not a maximum, because 100% acceptance means rubber-stamping, which is automation bias wearing a success metric's clothes; weekly "why did we…" questions answered from the brain; artifacts rendered per lens per month; Review Queue time-to-clear.

**Riskiest assumptions, each with its cheapest test:**

1. **PMs will actually triage a proposal queue weekly.** The whole model rests on this behavior. Test before writing product code: concierge it — hand-build a brain for three design partners from their real calls and tickets, send weekly proposal digests manually, and measure triage completion over four weeks.
2. **Insight-level proposals can be accurate enough that triage feels like judgment, not correction-slog.** Test offline: run the ingestion pipeline on real interview corpora and measure the correction rate a PM applies.
3. **Enterprises will connect the sources.** Permissioning must inherit source ACLs — an insight is visible only to people who could see its underlying signals — and that has to be designed early, not bolted on. Test: run one mid-market design partner's security questionnaire against the architecture on paper.
4. **The brain stays coherent as it grows** — duplicate insights, semantic merge conflicts, retrieval quality at scale. Test: seed a brain with six months of one team's historical data and measure duplication and retrieval precision.

**Open questions** (genuinely open): single-player first or team-visible from day one — whose brain is it, the PM's or the team's? Pricing unit — per-seat or per-brain? How much Jira/Linear state to mirror into the brain versus merely link out to?

---

*The one-sentence version: Cursor won because the repo already existed — this product wins by building the repo PMs never had, then making every gram of AI output arrive as a cited, reversible proposal against it.*

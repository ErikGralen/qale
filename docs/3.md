# The Product Brain: Concept Design for a PM Work OS

*Working draft — problem space first, then architecture. No UI design; precise product concepts only.*

---

## 1. Problem space: why there is no Cursor for PMs yet

Before designing anything, it's worth being honest about *why* Cursor worked for engineers, because the answer is not "AI got good at code."

Cursor inherited a substrate it never had to build. A developer's entire working reality is already **legible** (plain text), **unified** (one repo), **versioned** (git), and **verifiable** (compilers, tests, CI). An agent can read the full state of the world, propose a change as a *diff*, and the human can check it cheaply. The interaction loop — propose → review → verify → merge — existed before AI arrived. Cursor just put a model inside it.

PM work has none of these properties:

- **No repo.** Product truth is scattered across Jira/Linear, Slack, Notion/Confluence, Figma, call transcripts, support tickets, analytics, and — critically — people's heads. There is no single place where "the current state of the product effort" can be read.
- **No compiler.** There is no cheap, mechanical way to check whether a PRD claim, a roadmap assertion, or a summarized customer quote is *true*. Verification means hunting through five tools.
- **No diffs.** Product knowledge changes constantly (a decision gets reversed in a meeting, a customer churns, a metric moves) but the change is never captured as a reviewable delta. It just... happens, and everyone's mental model drifts.
- **Social and temporal context.** What matters to a PM is *what changed since Tuesday* and *who disagrees* — state deltas and people, not documents.

This explains the four pains that keep showing up in practitioner research and discovery interviews:

1. **Tool sprawl / the seams problem.** The highest-pain work isn't inside any one tool — it's synthesis *across* tools. No vendor owns the seams, so the PM is the integration layer, by hand.
2. **Missing workflow context.** Generic AI assistants don't know your product, your customers, your last decision, or your VP's pet concern. Every session starts from zero, so output is generic.
3. **The verification tax.** AI produces plausible text; the PM must then verify it against reality. If checking takes as long as writing, the AI saved nothing — and one hallucinated customer quote in an exec update destroys trust permanently.
4. **Role-identity anxiety.** PMs fear becoming rubber-stampers of machine-written PRDs. Any tool that positions AI as the author and the PM as the approver-of-slop will trigger rejection, however good the output.

**The job-to-be-done, stated properly:** the PM's core loop is not "write documents." It is *sense signals → form judgment → decide → align people → verify execution*. Documents are serializations of judgment for other people. Tools that only accelerate document production attack the exhaust, not the engine.

**Conclusion of the problem space:** the missing primitive is a legible, versioned, evidenced substrate for product knowledge. Cursor got its substrate for free; a PM Work OS must *construct* it. That constructed substrate is the Product Brain, and everything else in this design follows from it.

## 2. Design principles (each derived from a pain)

1. **Evidence or it doesn't exist.** Every fact and every generated claim links to its source — the Slack message, the transcript timestamp, the ticket, the metric query. Verification must take ten seconds, not twenty minutes. *(Kills the verification tax.)*
2. **The system proposes; the PM decides.** No content enters the Brain, no artifact ships, and nothing leaves the workspace without passing through human review. The PM is editor-in-chief; agents are staff. *(Defuses identity anxiety — and it is also just correct epistemics.)*
3. **One Brain, many views.** PRDs, roadmaps, updates, and briefs are *projections of the Brain*, not detached documents. Change the underlying fact once; every view that depends on it flags itself. *(Attacks the seams problem.)*
4. **Ambient by default, conversational on demand, never command-driven.** The system works in the background and surfaces results; the PM interacts through exactly three verbs — **Review, Ask, Approve**. No CLI, no slash commands, no prompt crafting. When the system needs input, *it* asks *you*, with tappable choices. The agent does the prompt engineering by interviewing the PM.
5. **Draft-only side effects.** The system never posts to Slack, edits Jira, or emails anyone autonomously. External writes are drafts awaiting one tap.
6. **Freshness is a first-class property.** Product knowledge decays. Every fact carries a last-verified date; stale facts get flagged, and artifacts built on stale facts warn about it.

## 3. The Product Brain (the core concept, precisely)

**Definition:** a versioned, evidence-linked model of everything the team knows and has decided about the product — the "repo" of product work.

### 3.1 What's in it

The Brain is a graph of typed entities. The v1 entity set:

| Entity | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | A raw inbound item: a customer quote, support ticket, transcript moment, survey row, metric alert, competitor move |
| **Theme** | A cluster of related Signals — an emerging pattern, pre-judgment |
| **Problem** | A validated pain, promoted from a Theme by the PM |
| **Bet** | A candidate solution to a Problem (feature, experiment, positioning move) |
| **Decision** | A choice made: what, why, alternatives considered, who approved, evidence at the time |
| **Goal** | A metric or outcome the team is driving |
| **Stakeholder** | A person: what they own, what they care about, where they stand on open Bets |
| **Release** | A shipped or planned milestone |
| **Risk** | A known threat with a trigger condition |
| **Open Question** | Something we don't know yet, with a plan to learn it |

Entities are connected by typed relations: a Signal *evidences* a Theme; a Bet *addresses* a Problem and *advances* a Goal; a Decision *supersedes* an earlier Decision; a Stakeholder *cares-about* a Bet. This is what makes "one Brain, many views" real — a roadmap is just a query over Bets and Releases; an exec update is a query over what changed.

**Open Question deserves emphasis:** it is the most PM-native entity in the model and no existing tool has it. A visible register of "what we don't know, and how we'll find out" is discovery practice turned into structure.

### 3.2 Every fact carries metadata

Each entity and each claim inside it has: **evidence links** (source, snippet, timestamp, deep link), a **confidence level**, a **last-verified date**, and an **author** (which agent or human wrote it). This metadata is what makes the Brain trustworthy rather than a hallucination cache.

### 3.3 How the Brain changes: Suggestions

Nothing writes directly to the Brain. Agents produce **Suggestions** — human-readable diffs like:

> *"New Theme: enterprise admins struggle with SSO provisioning — clustered from 14 Signals across 3 sources. Link to Problem P-12? [View evidence]"*

Suggestions land in one Review queue. The PM can **accept, edit, or reject** each one — the same interaction as Google Docs suggesting mode, which every PM already knows. Rejections with a reason are themselves signal: the Brain learns the PM's taste. *Your judgment is the training data* — this is the honest answer to identity anxiety, because the PM's accumulated editorial decisions become the org's most valuable asset.

**Auto-accept rules** give graduated autonomy without config files: simple toggles like "auto-accept new Signals from support tickets; always review anything touching Decisions." Trust is earned per category, at the PM's pace.

### 3.4 Mapping to the Value Chain

The Brain is the spine connecting all four stages of the Value Chain model (Signals → Insights → Artifacts → Alignment): ingestion agents populate the Signal layer; clustering produces Themes and Problems (Insights); artifacts are cited projections of the Brain; and the Stakeholder model plus tailored updates handle Alignment. Each stage needs a genuinely different AI capability — filtering, synthesis, drafting, communicating — which is exactly why this taxonomy was the right organizing principle.

## 4. Interaction model: three verbs, six surfaces, zero commands

PMs will not learn syntax. Every interaction in the product reduces to **Ask** (plain language, answered with citations), **Review** (accept/edit/reject a Suggestion or a tracked change), or **Approve** (release an external action). The surfaces:

1. **Briefing.** The start page. What changed since you last looked, what needs your judgment (Review queue count), what's going stale, what's at risk. Monday's Briefing is the anchor of the whole product (see §6).
2. **Review queue.** The inbox of Suggestions, each with a one-tap evidence panel. Designed to be clearable in minutes, on a phone, in a queue for coffee.
3. **Ask.** Free-form questions over the Brain: "What did enterprise customers say about onboarding this quarter?" Every sentence in the answer is clickable back to its source. Ask can also *do* work ("draft the Q3 update for the leadership channel") — output always arrives as a draft.
4. **Workbench.** The document editor. AI never regenerates a doc wholesale; it proposes **tracked changes**, exactly like a colleague in suggesting mode. Claims in documents are *live*: a stat pulled from the Brain updates or flags itself when the underlying fact changes.
5. **Entity pages.** Every Problem, Bet, Decision, and Stakeholder has a page showing its evidence, relations, and history — including "why do we believe this?", which is git-blame for product knowledge.
6. **Plays.** The packaged-workflow gallery (below).

### 4.1 Plays: how agent workflows ship without commands

A **Play** is a pre-built, named agent workflow that the PM configures through a short form — source pickers, audience, cadence, tone — never YAML, never prompts. Every Play shows a **preview on real data before it's enabled**, runs on a schedule or a tap, and always outputs drafts. Launch set:

- **Weekly Stakeholder Update** — drafts a cited update of what changed, per audience.
- **Interview Synthesis** — transcript in → Signals extracted, mapped to Themes, contradictions with existing beliefs flagged.
- **Competitive Watch** — monitors chosen competitors; Suggestions when something affects an active Bet.
- **Launch Readiness** — checks a Release against its open questions, risks, and unresolved decisions.
- **PRD from Problem** — assembles a draft PRD as a projection of an existing Problem, its evidence, and its Decisions, then opens it in Workbench in suggesting mode.

Every Play run produces a plain-language **activity trace** ("Read 42 messages in #customer-feedback → found 6 relevant → matched 4 to existing Themes → drafted 2 Suggestions"). Show the work — it's the difference between a trustworthy colleague and a black box.

### 4.2 Reverse prompting

When a Play or an Ask needs disambiguation, the system asks clarifying questions as tappable options ("Which audience is this update for: leadership / engineering / customers?"). The PM never composes a prompt. This single pattern removes the largest usability gap between technical and non-technical AI users.

## 5. Architecture: why a pi-style harness is the right engine

Here's the move that makes the whole design coherent: **store the Brain as plain markdown/YAML files in a git repository.** One file per entity, front-matter for metadata and relations, evidence as annotated links. The PM never sees a repo — but the system gets, for free:

- **Diffs** → Suggestions are literally git diffs rendered in human language.
- **History and blame** → "why do we believe this, since when, based on what" is native.
- **Branches** → see Scenario Branches below.
- **Portability and no lock-in** → the customer's Brain is their own readable files, a real enterprise trust point.

And it means you've translated PM work into the one domain where agent harnesses are already excellent: text files in a repo. That's why "something like pi.dev" fits. Pi is a minimal, MIT-licensed agent harness built around a four-tool core (read, write, edit, bash) with everything else — tools, skills, context management, scheduling — added through TypeScript extensions, and it can run headless via SDK/RPC modes for embedding in other products. Concretely:

- **Pi runs as an embedded engine**, never as an interface. The PM-facing product is a clean web/mobile app; pi sessions execute behind it.
- **Pi skills implement Plays.** A Play is a product-wrapped skill: markdown playbook + tools, loaded on demand. Internal iteration speed is high because improving a Play means editing a text file, not shipping app code.
- **Extensions implement connectors** (Slack, Jira/Linear, meeting transcripts, support, analytics — read-only OAuth first) plus Brain query tools and the permission gate.
- **A scheduler drives ambient runs**: nightly ingestion, Monday briefing assembly, freshness sweeps.
- **Sessions are the audit trail**, rendered as activity traces.
- Pi ships no built-in permission system, so each workspace's engine runs sandboxed with its own credentials, and **every external write routes through the Approve layer** — the harness is structurally incapable of posting anything the PM hasn't released.

One architectural warning: keep the harness swappable. The moat is the Brain schema, the connector layer, the Suggestion/review loop, and the accumulated judgment data — not the agent loop. Treat pi the way you'd treat a database engine.

### 5.1 Scenario Branches (the differentiator git makes nearly free)

Branch the Brain. "What if we cut SSO from Q3?" spins up a branch where the agent propagates consequences — which Goals slip, which Stakeholders are affected, which Decisions need revisiting, what the roadmap view looks like — presented side-by-side with main. Merge or discard. This is the PM equivalent of trying a refactor on a branch, it's genuinely hard for incumbents to copy without this substrate, and no PM tool on the market does it.

## 6. The wedge and sequencing

The Brain cannot be sold cold ("install our knowledge graph" is a dead pitch). It has to be *earned* as the byproduct of one painful job done visibly better.

**The wedge: the Monday loop.** Connect Slack + Jira/Linear + meeting transcripts. Ingestion runs all week. Monday morning: Briefing + a drafted, fully cited Weekly Stakeholder Update. PM reviews for ten minutes, edits, approves, sends. Why this wedge:

- Weekly cadence builds a habit loop with a natural retention metric.
- Status updates are high-pain, low-ego work — nobody's identity is threatened by automating them, unlike PRDs.
- It exercises the entire Value Chain in one loop, so it forces the whole architecture to exist in miniature.
- Its quality **visibly compounds** as the Brain grows — week 6 is obviously better than week 1, which is the retention moat and the demo.
- It's demoable in one screen to a buyer.

**Phase 2:** PRD Workbench (suggesting mode), Decision Log, Stakeholder model, Interview Synthesis Play.
**Phase 3:** Scenario Branches, roadmap projections, alignment tracking (who has seen/agreed with what), more connectors, team-level Brains.

**Non-goals (v1):** not a ticket system — Jira/Linear remain systems of record, this sits above them as the system of intelligence; not a Notion replacement; not autopilot — no unsupervised external actions, ever; no CLI, no command palette dependency, no user-visible prompts. Positioning line: *incumbents own the boxes; this owns the seams.* (Enterpret/Cycle own the feedback→insight slice; Rovo owns the Atlassian garden; the cross-tool Brain plus review loop is the uncovered ground.)

## 7. Trust mechanics (compressed)

Provenance click-through under ten seconds on every claim. Freshness decay with re-verification prompts surfaced in the Briefing ("3 things I believe may be stale"). Read-only connectors by default; write access unlocked per destination, per Play, after explicit opt-in. A complete audit log of every agent action. Onboarding via **Brain Bootstrap**: connect two or three sources, the agent proposes an initial Brain from the last 90 days, and the PM's first 30-minute review session doubles as the tutorial for the core interaction — plus a day-one wow ("ask anything about your own product history, with receipts").

---

## 8. Success metrics

**Leading:** ≥60% of Suggestions accepted without edit by week 4 (synthesis precision); median time-to-verify a cited claim <10s; weekly update production time from ~2 hours to <15 minutes; ≥70% of active PMs open the Monday Briefing four consecutive weeks.
**Lagging:** 3-month workspace retention; % of workspaces enabling a second Play within 60 days (expansion = brain trust); qualitative "would be very disappointed" score on the Monday loop.

## 9. Riskiest assumptions and cheapest tests

1. **PMs will do review work in exchange for compounding memory.** *The* kill-shot risk: if review feels like homework, the Brain rots and everything collapses. Cheapest test: a concierge/Wizard-of-Oz Monday loop — run it manually (or with pi behind the curtain) for five PMs for three weeks; measure whether they show up, whether drafts survive scrutiny, and whether they miss it when it stops.
2. **Orgs will grant connector access.** Test in the same concierge: does IT/security block read-only OAuth to Slack and Jira at target-size companies?
3. **Cross-source clustering is good enough** that Suggestions feel like insight, not noise. Measurable directly as acceptance rate during the concierge.
4. **The update is the right wedge** (vs. interview synthesis). The concierge can A/B this too: offer half the pilot PMs synthesis-first instead.

If assumption 1 survives three weeks of contact with real PMs, build the loop for real. Everything else in this document is sequenced to be earnable after that.

# The cold start, the asking

Companion to `docs/cold-start-path.md`. Written 2026-09-06 after a second round of four
brainstorms: when Qale asks, what makes an ask worth it, how the PM learns what the product
does, and one adversary arguing that questions are a cost and Qale should give first. This doc
is the convergence. It changes the path in three places, marked below. Nothing is decided.

**Amended 2026-09-06 by `docs/cold-start-learning.md`:** the five ask kinds and the floors are
gone. The value test, the budget, the never-ask windows, the payback, the loop and Part 3 stand
and now apply to the apprentice's questions.

Erik's three questions: at what points do we ask, how do we make sure the asking is valuable,
and how do we make sure the PM becomes familiar with the value the product provides.

---

## The one rule under all three

**Give first. Ask only when the answer changes what Qale does next.**

The adversary's charge stuck: the path billed nine questions across three beats before the
memory held one thing the PM had asked for, and every answer wrote a verified line into a
research page they had never opened. Ten answers, nothing they could see. If Qale has read
enough to write the question, it has usually read enough to write the answer as a draft, and a
wrong draft the PM corrects in sixty seconds beats a correct interview that never happens.

So every beat now has three tiers, and the ask is the last:

- **Drawn.** Arithmetic off the index, rendered on screen, no commit, cannot be wrong. Costs
  nothing.
- **Silent.** A committed file with an Activity row and a revert. The write policy already
  allows a new page in Memory to land this way. Filing and labels are here today.
- **Proposal.** A card. Anything that claims, promises, rewrites or leaves the machine.
- **Ask.** A question. Only when no give can be made without the answer.

The epic bet and the coverage question are no longer asks. They are statements with a
correction handle, drawn at the top of "Your month":

> I am ranking Checkout Rewrite first. 23 tickets moved, named in 6 of 41 meetings. Something
> else?

> Everything I have read is Checkout Rewrite, billing and the Bergman migration. If your
> quarter has a fourth thing, it is not in anything I can see.

The ranking is applied while unanswered and shows up in the next brief. Silence writes nothing
verified. That is the boundary the adversary found and it holds: **statements are for rankings
and readings, questions are for creating a named page about a real person or company, for a
promise, for consent to read a system, and for fuel.** Those are the five kinds that survive.

### The budget

Asks nobody started (the spine's, the librarian's, the weekly pass's) share **one slot,
app-wide**. While one waits, nothing else asks. Today that rule exists for the librarian only.

- Per sitting: one unowed ask. Inside a conversation the PM opened: up to three.
- Per day: two unowed, the second only if the first was answered.
- Per week: five. No rollover.
- An answer buys the next ask in the same breath. New fuel (a first read, a batch settling)
  buys one, because the PM handed it over.
- An unanswered ask holds the slot. At 7 days it goes stale, the tick drops it, and that kind
  goes quiet for a week. Two stale in a row on one kind retires the kind for this workspace.
  That is the capture-mute rule, generalised.
- The getting-started session asks at most five questions over its life, one open at a time,
  and never before its give for that beat is on screen.

### The three moments Qale never asks

1. **Around a meeting.** From the 60-minute prep lead until 15 minutes after the end, no unowed
   ask. Give the brief, then be silent. A card at 09:58 is the worst screen we can draw.
2. **The first open after three or more days away.** Monday 08:40 the app owes them the week:
   the tally, today's meetings, the brief. The parked question waits for the second open that
   day or the next real event. Never a word about the gap.
3. **Mid-flight.** A run going, a card open, a drop in progress, the editor focused. The
   question parks and surfaces as a row when things are idle. Nothing for a day after they
   declined something.

### Steady state

After the spine retires: capture on an empty meeting, the librarian on a finding that held
still, the weekly pass on fuel, the claim check when a brief leaned on something old, and
whatever the PM starts. Meeting-prep never asks: it briefs or stays silent. Most days zero
unowed asks. A week with none is correct.

## Part 2. What makes an ask worth it

### The value test

A question is asked only if it passes all seven. The facts pass supplies the fact, the skill
copy supplies the sentence.

1. It carries a name, a number and a date off the index that the PM can check in one glance.
2. It tells the PM something before it asks anything, so it pays even if they never answer.
3. **It names its payoff.** "If yes, I track these five and brief you before Thursday." No
   payoff, no question. **This is the criterion every question fails today.**
4. It is answered in one tap or four words. Rows for a set, two options for a bet, and "you
   are reading that wrong" beside them.
5. Neither the workspace nor a default can answer it.
6. Nothing else from this session is waiting.
7. A no writes as much as a yes. If only yes writes, it is a form with a nicer face.

The interview's forks ("tell me in your own words / drop something in / skip") fail test 2.
They teach Qale and tell the PM nothing. They stay, but they never open a sitting.

### The payback, within ten seconds

Every answer ends in the closing-beat receipt: what was written, where, the door, and Put
back. Never a thank-you.

- **Yes.** The claim lands verified by them, still citing the source, `asked: true`, so it
  lands silently with a commit and an Activity row. Receipt: "Written: Checkout Rewrite is
  your main thing this quarter, in the product page. Next: a brief before Thursday's review."
- **No, or corrected.** The corrected claim lands the same way, and the source line keeps the
  disagreement: "Jira says In Progress; you say parked until Q4." If the correction is about
  how to read a source ("In Progress on our board means parked"), the session also proposes a
  rule on the Jira conventions skill. That is a card, because it changes every future read.
- **Not now.** Nothing lands. One line: "Skipped. I ask again only if these tickets change."
  The check ledger records the kind and an evidence fingerprint (the ticket ids, the person,
  the domain), and the facts pass skips the question while the fingerprint is unchanged.
- **Stop asking this.** A third door on every question card. It proposes a house-rules
  bullet: "Do not ask about stalled tickets." Approving it is the last time they see the kind.

A promise confirmed is its own card, a person or customer page is a proposal, a corrected
hypothesis is a good answer and never feeds the stop loop.

The PM sees why a question was asked: the evidence line is the why. Never a floor, never a
"Why this" fold. A question that needs an explainer failed test 1.

### The loop, and why each mechanism

- A skip goes to the **ledger**, keyed by kind and fingerprint. "Did the inputs change" is a
  context-free fact, so a gate is allowed. Same pattern as the librarian's declined findings.
- A correction of a reading goes to **skill copy** through the existing standing-instruction
  path. A rule the model reads changes the next reading, not just the next question, and the
  PM can edit it on the skill page. A ledger cannot hold "In Progress means parked".
- Stop asking goes to **house rules**, copy again, because a preference is applied with
  judgement.
- Floors are arithmetic and stay fixed per install. They move from telemetry across installs,
  never from one PM's skips.
- Repeat skips need no counter: one sentence in the skill says that after three skips of one
  kind, the next question of that kind offers to stop, with the rule as the yes.

### Three numbers that say questions earn their cost

1. **Answer rate by kind.** New `ask.decided` with kind (a closed set: stalled, promise,
   domain, fuel, consent, interview, claim, librarian), decision (answered, corrected, skipped,
   stopped), age band, offered flag. A kind under half gets rewritten; a kind stopped by a
   fifth of installs gets deleted.
2. **Writes per answer.** `ask.decided` carries a band of what landed in the same turn. A
   question that lands nothing is a form, and this number names it.
3. **Verified claims on day 7 against day 1** on `memory.measured`. If questions earn, this
   moves. If it stays flat while writes per answer is high, the PM is confirming things that
   never matter later.

Plus the adversary's number: **gives opened before the first question was answered.** If that
is low, the gives are not being seen and the asking is carrying the product alone.

## Part 3. How the PM learns what the product does

No tour, no tooltips, no checklist. Six behaviours seen once each, on their own material, at a
moment they were already looking. Five happen on day one.

### The six sights, in the order the path produces them

1. **A page about their month they did not write.** Minute 3. "Your month", drawn from the
   calendar. The first time they learn the app looked before it spoke.
2. **A question with a source.** Same minute, the top row of Next: a person and a number they
   can check against their own calendar, and a payoff line under it.
3. **A filed transcript matched to its meeting.** Minute 5. "Filed 12. Matched 9 to meetings
   on your calendar, 3 I could not place." Named counts, not a spinner. "Source" stops being a
   button label and becomes a thing that lands somewhere.
4. **A silent write with a revert.** A beat later. One pointer from the batch receipt to
   Activity, once: each transcript is a row, "Filed the Nordkap review, matched to 12 Aug",
   with Put back. This is the sight that proves the policy's core claim.
5. **A citation they can click.** The promise question: the meeting name is a live link to the
   passage. "Cite or decline" as a felt thing.
6. **A proposal with a diff.** The product picture, or the seed card: a change waiting for
   their yes rather than a fact stated as done.
7. **A brief that arrived unasked.** Day 2 or later, behind the meeting-prep gate. A prep
   section on the meeting page they were about to open anyway, citing what it used. The sight
   that pays off the week: value they did not ask for, where they already were.

### The four words, taught in use

- **source**: the Add source button, minute 2:30, and sight 3 gives it a place to land.
- **session**: never defined. It arrives backward, from the Activity row's link "in the
  session", the first time a silent write traces to a conversation.
- **proposal**: the shape (a diff, Approve, Discard) carries the meaning before the word. Its
  first appearance as a word is the day-2 row, "3 proposals from yesterday's read", after
  they have approved one.
- **memory**: the daily tally footer, from day 2: "Your memory holds 31 meetings, 9 with
  notes, 14 people, 6 tracked tickets, 3 things you confirmed." A count, not a metaphor, and
  the last word taught because it only means something once the other three have put
  something in it.

### Proof, not tour

Three surfaces, three tenses. **The tally** is now: on Next every day, counts only, no
adjectives, true on day one even when small. **The receipt** is just now: after every session
that shipped anything, naming the actual note, linked, never "2 changes". **Activity** is ever:
sentences in the agent's own voice, each revertable, so a PM who never opens it in week one can
open it in week six and read what happened without them.

### The colleague test

After week one the PM should be able to say: "It reads my meetings and Jira and asks me the
things I would otherwise forget." A sentence about behaviour, not category.

The events that show they can: `ask.decided` answered at least once, `card.decided` with a
first flag, `memory.measured` on day 7 above day 1 on the same install, and an Activity revert
either used or left alone past its window. The test is passed by the log, never asked of the
PM.

## What this changes in the path

Three reversals of earlier decisions, each needing Erik's call.

1. **Person pages from the calendar land silently.** Today the calendar refuses to create a
   person page (people-chips, 2026-07-28), so "who you meet" is a fact Qale holds and will not
   write down. An arithmetic page (name, email, domain, meetings attended, first and last seen)
   for an attendee at three or more meetings cannot be wrong, only thin, and the policy already
   lets a new Memory page land. Claims about a person still ask.
2. **The product picture is drafted from the sources, as one unverified proposal.** This bends
   product-understanding U-2 ("the sources are the check, not the author") further than the
   first-look amendment did. Every line cited, thin areas left visibly blank, and the interview
   shrinks to the gaps. "Confident mush" is answered by blank gaps, not by refusing to draft.
3. **The ladder shrinks to five kinds, and rankings become statements.** Dead tickets, the
   external relationship, follow consent, a promise, and fuel. The seed card folds into the
   questions that produced it rather than being a second yes.

And four additions to the build list:

- The facts pass also renders: "Your month" and the three Jira lists, drawn, no model, no
  cards. About two days on top of the pass.
- One unowed-ask slot app-wide with the budget, the calendar quiet window off the agenda
  query the prep sweep already runs, a last-open stamp for the three-day rule, and ask-kind
  rows in the check ledger for staleness. About two days.
- The question card gains a payoff line, a "Stop asking this" door routed to the standing-
  instruction path, and the closing-beat receipt after an ask lands writes. About two days.
- A second meeting-prep beat, 15 minutes after a meeting, only when the brief cited a claim
  over 30 days old. One day.

## Decisions this doc needs

1. Give-first as the rule: drawn and silent tiers before any ask, per beat.
2. Silent arithmetic person pages from the calendar (reverses the 2026-07-28 rule).
3. The product picture drafted from sources as one unverified proposal (bends U-2).
4. The five surviving ask kinds, with the epic bet and coverage as statements.
5. The budget numbers: one unowed slot, two a day, five a week, 7-day expiry, two stale
   retires a kind.
6. The three never-ask windows, including "the first open after three days away gives only".

# Question bank

The long form. The guide is the five rules; these are the sentences to actually say, grouped by
what you are trying to learn. Read the group you need, not the whole file.

## Last real occurrence

- When did this last happen? Walk me through that day.
- What were you doing right before you hit it?
- What did you do instead? How long did that take?
- Who else was waiting on you while you did it?
- How often does that week look like that one?

## Symptom vs job

- If this worked perfectly tomorrow, what would you stop doing?
- Who reads the output? What do they do with it?
- What happens if the number is wrong? Who finds out, and when?
- Is this the job, or a step on the way to a job you would rather describe?
- Which part is the annoying part: getting the data, trusting it, or sending it on?

## Size of the pain

- How many people on your side hit this in a month?
- What does an hour of that person's time cost you, roughly?
- What is the worst outcome you have had from it so far?
- Has anyone built a workaround? Who maintains it?
- What did you try before talking to us?

## Behind a feature request

- What made you land on that shape? Did you see it somewhere?
- Suppose we could not build it. What would you do?
- Which part of it would you drop if you had to ship half?
- Who asked you for it, and what did they say in their own words?

## Buying and blockers

- Who else has to say yes? What do they care about?
- What has stopped a purchase like this before, here?
- What is the deadline, and what happens the day after it passes?
- Is there a security or procurement review, and how long does it usually run?

## What to avoid

- Do not ask whether they would use a feature. Nobody knows, and everybody says yes.
- Do not ask them to rank a list you wrote. You will learn about your list.
- Do not accept a number without asking how they arrived at it.
- Do not fill silence. The sentence after the pause is usually the real one.

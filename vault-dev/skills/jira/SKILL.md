Qale follows these rules when it drafts for Jira. Edit them freely; short imperative bullets work
best. Keep them high level: what the team wants done, not a copy of the Jira setup.

Read from your last twelve tickets in BOK, GST and PAY on 2026-07-10. A guess from your tickets,
not a rule you gave me. Change any line and I draft the new way from the next ticket on.

## When you draft a ticket

- File it under `BOK` (Bookings), `GST` (Guest) or `PAY` (Payments), whichever team owns the work.
  Payments is Henrik's team, so a ticket there is a request to them: say so in the description.
- Give it one area label: `no-show-fees`, `waitlist`, `group-bookings`, `reminders`,
  `booking-page` or `payments`. One label, never two.
- If the work comes from one customer, name them in the first line of the description
  ("Brasserie Lund needs this before Christmas bookings open on 1 November.").
- A story under an epic sets the epic as its parent. BOK-300 (no-show fees) and BOK-260
  (waitlist) are the ones with stories today.

## Labels

- `no-show-fees`: the fee epic and its stories: the card at booking, the charge after a no-show.
  2 of 12 tickets.
- `waitlist`: joining the list from the booking page, and texting the first guest when a table
  frees. 3 of 12.
- `group-bookings`: parties over eight, set menu, deposit. 1 of 12.
- `reminders`: the day-before text and whatever sends it. 1 of 12.
- `booking-page`: what a guest sees when they book, the Google button and table areas included.
  3 of 12.
- `payments`: card storage and deposits, the Payments team's work. 2 of 12.
- One area label per ticket, never two.

## When you comment

- Say what changed and what happens next. The ticket already says what the work is.
- Put a date on anything that is expected, and name who owes it ("Target: end of October, told
  Brasserie Lund on 2026-07-16").
